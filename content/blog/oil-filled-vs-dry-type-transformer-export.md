Both types do the same job. The right choice depends on where the transformer will stand, how hot it gets there, and what the local fire rules say.

## Oil-filled: the outdoor workhorse

An oil-filled transformer is cooled and insulated by mineral or ester oil, with radiators on the tank.

- **Best for:** outdoor substations, solar parks, industrial yards, utility distribution.
- **Strengths:** lower cost per kVA, handles overloads well, and copes with hot, dusty sites when the design ambient is specified correctly.
- **Watch for:** oil containment pits, fire separation distances, and hermetically sealed or conservator designs depending on the utility.
- **Ratings:** from small distribution units up to large power transformers with on-load tap changers.

## Dry-type: indoor and close to people

A dry-type (cast resin) transformer has windings cast in epoxy, with no liquid.

- **Best for:** buildings, hospitals, data centres, metros and anywhere the transformer sits indoors near people.
- **Strengths:** low fire risk, no oil to contain, less maintenance.
- **Watch for:** higher cost, an enclosure for protection (IP rating) and good ventilation in hot rooms.

## Specify the grid, not just the kVA

For export, the rating is only half the specification. Give the primary and secondary voltage, frequency (50 or 60 Hz), vector group, standard (IEC 60076 or the local equivalent), design ambient and nameplate language. Saudi Arabia runs at 60 Hz with a 13.8 kV or 33 kV primary; Nigeria and Kenya run at 50 Hz with 11 kV or 33 kV.
